Getting started

This is a catalogue of the oldest buildings and structures in the British Isles: the United Kingdom, Ireland, the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands. It holds everything begun before 1500, the oldest surviving example of each type of building, and landmark buildings of any date (Grade I, Category A, World Heritage Sites and other widely known structures), plus tens of thousands of places of interest and the people connected with them.

{{ shot('catalogue.jpg', 'The catalogue: filters on the left, results as a gallery, table, map or timeline.') }}
  1. Type in the search box (/): a name, a town, a saint, a person ("Shakespeare").
  2. Narrow down with the filters: building group, period, nation, county, heritage grade, date basis.
  3. Click a card to open the building; use ☆ to add it to the trip planner, ⇄ to compare.
  4. Press ⌘K (Ctrl+K on Windows/Linux) anywhere to jump to a page, building, place or person.

Catalogue

Views: Gallery (photos), Table (sortable columns), Map (colour by building group or period, "only what's on the map" filter) and Timeline (how many buildings were begun per century, 50, 500 or 1000 years). Near a place: type a town or postcode, or use your location, then choose a distance; sort by "Nearest first". Copy link keeps every filter, so a shared link opens the same view. Dates marked ≈ are estimates (see Date basis).

A building's page

{{ shot('detail.jpg', "A building's page: photo, dates with their basis, map with outline, floor plan, historic maps, people and nearby places.") }}

Each record shows when it was built and how we know (recorded, from the register's description, from a Wikipedia article, or estimated from the type), a date check against independent sources, its designations, address, architect and style, and a "Sources & references" box listing every source and the references Wikidata attaches to each fact. Below the map: the building's outline from OpenStreetMap, a floor plan where one exists (which can be laid over the map), historic maps of this place (scanned, georeferenced maps on Allmaps, most detailed first), people connected with it, nearby old buildings and places of interest. 🗺 Show on the map opens the Map page zoomed in on the record, ringed and named (for a place of interest its layer is switched on). Locally you can correct a record; corrections survive rebuilds.

Map

{{ shot('map_layers.jpg', 'The Map page with the layer switcher open: background, map layers and historic maps.') }}

Buildings by group plus layers of places (battles, archaeology, lost places, gardens and trees, industrial sites, finds, plaques, history events). The ☰ Layers button on every map chooses the background (online street map, satellite imagery from Esri with place names (local app), or the bundled offline map), the offline layers (counties, national parks and AONBs, rivers and canals, lakes, major roads, minor roads and streams (off by default; shown when zoomed in), railways and stations, historic counties (before 1974), national trails, regional and local walking routes, the National Cycle Network and regional cycle routes, Roman roads, buried (culverted) rivers, towns, hills and coast, airports; borders, national parks, routes, trails, Roman roads, buried rivers and stations also show over the street map) and a historic map with an opacity slider: Ordnance Survey six-inch and one-inch, Roy's military survey, the Gough Map, William Smith's 1815 geological map, historical atlases, town plans and the Ordnance Survey six-inch maps of Ireland (first edition, 1829–42, and a later revision). Zoomed in, building outlines appear.

Places

{{ shot('places.jpg', 'Places of interest: battles, monuments, gardens, plaques, finds and more, as a list or a map.') }}

Places that are not buildings: battlefields, archaeological sites from the official registers, holy wells, hill figures, Roman roads, deserted villages, shipwrecks, registered gardens, notable trees, industrial sites, finds and hoards, objects kept in buildings, commemorative plaques (Wikidata and Open Plaques) and located history events. Filter by layer, type, nation, period and source.

People

People linked to buildings and places: buried, born, lived, worked or died there; architects, founders, owners, those commemorated by plaques and those who took part in battles. Where Wikidata gives a source for a connection, it is shown next to it.

Pronunciation

{{ shot('pron_detail.jpg', 'How to say a name: variety, IPA, respelling with the stressed syllable in capitals, and a recording.') }}

Under the name of a building, place or person you may see how it is said, and its name in other languages of the isles. Each line gives the variety (English, local, Scottish English, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Irish with its Munster / Connacht / Ulster dialects, Manx, Scots, Cornish, Jèrriais…), the IPA transcription between slashes, a respelling in capitals for the stressed syllable ("BEE-vər"), and ▶ to play a recording. "Saying <town>" lines are for the town or area the record lies in. The Pronunciation page lists them all, by variety, and exports them as CSV or JSON. Sources: the opening of the English Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA) and Wikidata (CC0), whose recordings are on Wikimedia Commons, many from the Lingua Libre project; each recording's author and licence are on its file page. Turn pronunciations or other-language names off in Settings.

History

Periods from the Neolithic to the modern era, a timeline of sourced events (each checked against its source; see Data quality) and where they happened.

Oldest by type

For about fifty building types (castle, church, pub, bridge, lighthouse, theatre…) the oldest surviving examples, with the claim that is usually made and the evidence behind it.

Below them, researched lists of the oldest things that are not buildings: ancient trees, rocks and fossils, the earliest human traces; burials, gravestones, churchyards and cemeteries; markets, fairs, shops, firms and works; roads, waterworks, railways, canals, docks and communications. Each entry has its sources and a verdict on the claim (confirmed, corrected or open). The earliest dated places of each new kind follow, marked as unchecked Wikidata dates.

Stories & legends

Ghosts and the supernatural, legends and myths, folklore and customs, and curious history (secret passages, priest holes, treasure, smugglers) told about the buildings and places, taken from their Wikipedia articles with a link back. They appear under Stories & legends on a building's or place's page, on the Stories page (by theme and nation, with a map), and as a Stories filter in the catalogue sidebar. Legends are told as legends: they record what people believed or said.

Tours

Ready-made tours: walks around historic towns, bike rides and day trips by car, for all sights or a theme (ghosts & legends, prehistoric, castles, early churches, Roman). Routes follow real paths and roads (OpenStreetMap, OSRM); the time includes a stop at each place. Each tour opens in Google Maps for turn-by-turn directions, downloads as GPX (sat-navs, cycling and walking apps) or KML (Google Earth), prints, or copies to Your tour.

Your tour: star (☆) buildings and places anywhere, or ✨ Suggest a tour from a town, postcode, your location or the map centre, for one to two hours, half a day or a day, on a theme. Choose walking, cycling or driving and whether to return to the start; Best order finds the shortest order; + Stops along the way shows notable places close to the route to slot in. Save as many tours as you like (kept in this browser; Settings → Backup saves them to a file). Share link puts the stops in a link you can send.

Long-distance and pilgrim ways: national trails, Scotland's Great Trails, Irish waymarked ways and pilgrim routes (St Cuthbert's Way, the Fife Pilgrim Way…) as multi-day walks, with the sights near the path and day stages of about 20 km. Walks and rides show an elevation profile with the climb (from a 25 m terrain model, so short climbs are smoothed out).

On the way: 📍 Follow shows where you are, the next stop, its distance and direction, and ticks stops off as you reach them. 🔊 Listen to the tour reads out each stop and its story. Building pages show the nearest railway or bus stations ("Getting there") and, where OpenStreetMap knows them, who looks after the place, opening hours and whether entry is free; check before you go.

On this day, timelines and the quiz

The History page opens with On this day: battles, buildings founded or opened, and births and deaths of the people in the catalogue on today's date (‹ › for other days). Dates before 1752 are given in the calendar used at the time. A building's page has a Timeline of dated events from its article: built, rebuilt, besieged, burned, dissolved, restored. The Quiz shows ten photographed buildings: guess the period each was built in.

On the Places page and the map, Legends & literature adds reportedly haunted places, places of legend (Arthurian sites, fairy forts, Avalon, Lyonesse, Cantre'r Gwaelod: shown where tradition puts them, as claims), holy wells, and the real places where notable novels, plays and poems are set. The Literary places and Ghosts & legends tours use them, with the homes and graves of writers.

Maritime heritage adds harbours and ports, docks, shipyards, ropewalks, custom houses, lighthouses, lifeboat stations, museum ships and shipbuilders. Industrial heritage now covers the Industrial Revolution (mills, mines, ironworks, canals, railways, engine houses, kilns, iron bridges, 1700–1914) and Victorian era its public buildings, piers and glasshouses (1837–1901). Each has tours.

Underground gathers what lies below ground: tube, subway and metro stations with their opening years, closed "ghost" stations (Aldwych, Down Street, British Museum), railway, canal, road and foot tunnels (Thames Tunnel, Box, Standedge, Severn), air-raid and deep-level shelters, Cold War bunkers and Royal Observer Corps posts, caves and mines, ice houses, grottoes, catacombs and Bazalgette's sewers. The map layer Underground railways & tunnels (☰ Layers) draws the tube lines and tunnels; there are Underground tours.

Military heritage covers defences through the ages after the castles: the Saxon burhs of the Burghal Hidage, Henry VIII's Device Forts, Martello towers, the Palmerston Forts, batteries, barracks and drill halls, and the works of the world wars and the Cold War: pillboxes and anti-tank defences, stop lines, radar stations and sound mirrors, airfields and prisoner-of-war camps, and war memorials. The map layer Pillboxes, bunkers & trenches shows several thousand smaller works mapped on OpenStreetMap. Tours: Coastal defences and Military heritage.

Museums & collections lists museums of every kind (national, local, regimental, transport, science, historic houses), art galleries, and libraries and archives, closed ones marked as closed. A museum's page has Kept here: its best-known objects (the Rosetta Stone and the Lewis chessmen at the British Museum, the Arnolfini Portrait at the National Gallery), and finds and hoards link to the museum that holds them. There are Museums & galleries town walks.

Bridges gathers the bridges on Wikidata by type: clapper and packhorse bridges and stepping stones, aqueducts, swing and bascule bridges, suspension and cable-stayed bridges, railway viaducts, footbridges and road bridges, with the year each opened. The bridges begun before 1500 and the famous ones are in the catalogue as well. There are Bridges tours.

Excavations shows where archaeologists have dug: sites with recorded excavations and every Time Team dig, with what the team looked for. A building or place that has been excavated says when and by whom (Excavated: under "Getting there"), and Finds & treasures now includes the hoards in Wikipedia's lists, with where each was found, when, what it held and the museum that keeps it. Find-spots of metal-detected finds are kept vague on purpose by the Portable Antiquities Scheme, so only published hoards are shown. Tours: Archaeology & digs.

Offline use and installing

The offline map (☰ Layers → Offline map) works without internet: counties and historic counties, roads (minor roads and streams when zoomed in), lakes, rivers, trails, stations and towns. In the local app, ⬇ Save this area for offline in the Layers panel keeps the street map of the area in view (zoom 10–15) in the data folder, for a walk or a tour; Settings → Maps lists and deletes saved areas. The local app can be installed from the browser (Install / Add to Home Screen); pages, tours and the places you opened then also work offline.

Data quality

Where the data is incomplete or sources disagree: undated records, date conflicts (with the conflicting sentence quoted), missing photos or coordinates, and the research claims checked against their sources. Links go straight to the Wikidata item, so gaps can be fixed at the source.

On a record's page, Evidence lists each researched claim with its sources (primary source, heritage listing, secondary or reference work), the exact quote and whether the last check found it in the source; tree-ring dates and the Domesday Book entry follow.

{{ shot('evidence.jpg', 'Evidence on Canterbury Cathedral: Bede and the list entry, tree-ring dates of its roofs, and Domesday Book.') }}

Rebuild and other jobs (local app): rebuild the database, find photos, outlines and floor plans, offline map layers, the hosted copy, and packaging the database for a new installation. Each shows its stage, a progress bar, downloads vs cache hits and the time left (estimated from the last run). Jobs keep running if you close the page or the app; reopen the Data quality page to follow them, or Stop one. Downloads are cached, so a rebuild reuses them; to pick up newer Wikidata or Wikipedia edits, open the rebuild options and download again what is older than some days, or everything from chosen sources.

Settings

{{ shot('settings.jpg', 'Settings: appearance, catalogue and map defaults, units, history, data location, backup.') }}

Appearance: theme (system, light, dark; or press T), text size, compact layout, reduced motion. Catalogue: how it opens (view, sort, results per page) and distance units. Maps: default background and marker clustering. History & shortcuts: recently viewed records, single-key shortcuts. Data location (local app): where the download cache and the database are kept, with the free space on each drive. Move or copy them to an external drive, back to the project folder, or any folder; the app checks every file before switching and deleting the old copy, and tells you if a drive is unplugged. Backup: save your trip list, compare list, map layers and preferences to a file and load them elsewhere.

Glossary

Date built
When construction began (the earliest surviving fabric where sources say so).
Date basis
Recorded: Wikidata or a curated Wikipedia list. From register description: the official listing text (Historic Environment Scotland, Cadw). From Wikipedia article: mined from the article, with the sentence kept as evidence. Estimate (building type): the accepted date range for that kind of monument. Not recorded: no date found. Anything but "Recorded" shows ≈.
Date check
Corroborated: another source agrees within 100 years (500 for prehistoric dates). Referenced: the date carries a reference on Wikidata. Single source: nothing independent to compare. Conflict: another source is more than 150 years away; worth a look.
Landmark
Included regardless of date: Grade I (England, Wales), Category A (Scotland), Grade A (Northern Ireland), World Heritage Sites, structures with articles in many languages, Crown Dependency heritage.
Oldest of its type
Among the earliest surviving examples of a building type, from Wikidata and researched claims.

Questions

Why is a building missing?

The catalogue starts from Wikidata and the official registers. A building without a Wikidata item, or without a date before 1500 and not a landmark, will not appear. Adding the date on Wikidata brings it in at the next rebuild.

A date looks wrong. What can I do?

Open the record, check the date basis and evidence, and use "Correct this record" (local app) or fix it on Wikidata (link on the record).

Why are some photos "taken nearby"?

Records without a photo get the nearest geotagged Wikimedia Commons photo; the caption says when it may show the surroundings rather than the building itself.

The map is blank or grey.

The online street map needs internet. Choose "Offline map" under ☰ Layers, or make it the default in Settings.

The app says the data folder is not available.

The download cache or database is on a drive that is not connected. Plug it in, or choose another location in Settings → Data location (Use a copy that is already there).

Starting on a new computer.

Without a database the app opens on its setup page: use the bundled database (downloaded from the project's GitHub release), point it at data you already have (a drive with the cache and/or database), or build from scratch (hours, in the background). From the terminal: ./run.sh --data /Volumes/MyDrive, --bundled or --build.

Does anything I do here leave my computer?

Searches and preferences stay in your browser. Looking up a place name or postcode asks postcodes.io / OpenStreetMap; maps and photos load from their servers.
